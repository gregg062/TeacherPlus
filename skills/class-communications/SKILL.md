# Class communications

## General style

- Warm, welcoming, plain language (around a 6th-8th grade reading level for families); short paragraphs, headings, bullet points.
- Lead with what families most need (dates, what to do, who to contact).
- Avoid jargon and acronyms; explain any education terms.
- Offer a translated version for families who prefer another language and suggest a school interpreter check it.

## Templates

- **Syllabus**: course description, what students will learn (big ideas), materials, grading breakdown and policies (late work, retakes, missing work), classroom expectations, how to get help, communication preferences and teacher contact, and a signature/acknowledgment section if wanted. Keep it to 2 pages; offer a one-page student-friendly version.
- **Welcome letter**: a bit about the teacher, what the year will look like, how families can help, how to contact; optionally a short "tell me about your child" question list.
- **Newsletter** (weekly/monthly): what we learned, what's coming up, how to help at home (one concrete idea), important dates, celebrations. 300 words max.
- **Back-to-school night**: 8-10 slides with speaker notes, 10-15 minutes: welcome, about me, a day in our class, what we'll learn, grading, how to support at home, communication, questions.
- **Field trip / permission letter**: purpose and learning connection, date and times, location, transportation, cost and financial help available, what to bring, lunch, chaperones, medical/allergy info, return-by date, tear-off permission slip with emergency contact.

## Rules

- Check dates and days of the week match.
- Don't include individual student information in whole-class messages or photos without permission.
