# Observation & walkthrough feedback

Feedback is for the teacher's growth. It must be **evidence-based** (what was seen and heard, not judgments), **bite-sized** (one main next step) and **actionable this week**.

## From notes to feedback

1. Ask (only if not clear): the focus of the observation (if any agreed), the observed teacher's experience level, whether the feedback is formal (evaluation) or informal (coaching), and the framework the school uses, if any.
2. Separate the notes into **evidence** (low-inference: quotes, actions, counts, times — "12 of 25 students raised hands", "teacher talked for 18 minutes") and **interpretation**. Feedback should lead with evidence.
3. Write:
   - **Praise** — one or two specific strengths with evidence and their impact on students.
   - **Probe** — a reflective question that helps the teacher notice the growth area themselves.
   - **Next step** — **one** high-leverage, observable action ("Plan three cold-call questions and use them after think time"), with why it matters for students.
   - **Practice plan** — how the teacher will practice it before using it (rehearsal, script, modeled example), and when the coach will check back.
   - **Evidence** section — the notes that support each point.
4. Keep it under ~300 words for informal feedback. For formal evaluations, map evidence to the framework's components without inventing ratings — the evaluator decides scores.

## Walkthrough forms

When asked, create a one-page walkthrough tool with 4-6 look-fors tied to the school's focus (e.g. objective posted and referenced, students talking about content, checks for understanding, student work at standard level), with space for low-inference evidence — not a checklist of yes/no compliance items.

## Rules

- Never include personal judgments about the teacher's character or comparisons with colleagues.
- Don't include student names; describe students by seat or "a student".
- Formal evaluation decisions belong to the evaluator; flag anything that could be a contractual/HR issue for the teacher's administrator instead of writing it into coaching feedback.
