# Department & team leadership

## Meeting agendas

Every agenda has: a purpose (one sentence), desired outcomes, timed items with an owner, a protocol for any discussion, and decisions/action items with names and dates at the end. Put the most important item first, keep announcements to email, and fit the real meeting length. Include a short norms reminder.

## Collaborative protocols (pick and script one)

- **Looking at student work**: describe the work (what do you see?) → interpret (what does it tell us about student thinking?) → implications for teaching → next steps. Timed, with a facilitator script.
- **Data meeting**: what do we notice? → what do we wonder? → root causes we control → one action per teacher → when we'll check results.
- **Tuning protocol** (feedback on a lesson/assessment): presenter context → clarifying questions → warm and cool feedback → presenter reflection.
- **Grading calibration**: everyone scores the same 3-4 anonymized samples with the rubric independently → compare → discuss disagreements until there's a shared understanding → write anchor notes for each level.

## Common assessments

Plan by: agreeing on the standards → blueprint (see `assessment-builder`) → writing and reviewing items together (see `assessment-review`) → administration window → shared scoring/calibration → data meeting.

## Department plans and goals

Write goals as SMART goals tied to student outcomes, with actions, owners, timelines, resources and how progress will be measured. For requests to administration (budget, resources, schedule), write a concise case: the need, the evidence, the ask, the cost, and the expected impact on students.

## Rules

- Use anonymized student work and aggregate data in department meetings.
- Keep a neutral, collegial tone; department heads lead peers, so emphasize shared ownership.
