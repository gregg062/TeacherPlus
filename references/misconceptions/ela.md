# English language arts — common misconceptions

Status: DRAFT, awaiting educator review.

## Reading
- **Theme is a single word ("friendship").** Response: theme is a statement about life the text suggests ("True friendship requires sacrifice"); topic vs. theme sort.
- **Main idea = the first sentence / the most interesting detail.** Response: "What is most of the paragraph about?"; test candidate main ideas against each detail.
- **Summarizing means retelling everything** (or including opinions). Response: somebody-wanted-but-so-then frames; word limits; objective language.
- **Inference is a guess.** Response: inference = evidence from the text + what I know; always cite the clue.
- **The narrator is the author.** Response: point of view lessons; unreliable narrators.
- **Citing evidence = dropping a quote.** Response: quote sandwich: introduce, quote, explain how it supports the claim.

## Writing
- **A claim is a fact or a topic.** Response: a claim is arguable; "Could a reasonable person disagree?"
- **Counterclaims weaken an argument.** Response: addressing a counterclaim and rebutting it strengthens credibility.
- **Longer is better / five paragraphs is the rule.** Response: structure follows purpose; quality of reasoning matters more than length.
- **Revising = fixing spelling.** Response: revision (ideas, organization, evidence) vs. editing (conventions); separate passes.
- **Paraphrasing = changing a few words.** Response: read, cover, write from memory, then check; cite paraphrases too.

## Grammar and conventions
- **Commas go wherever you pause.** Response: teach specific comma rules (lists, introductory elements, compound sentences with a conjunction).
- **Never start a sentence with "because" or "and".** Response: a style preference, not a rule; fragments are the actual problem.
- **Its/it's, their/there/they're.** Response: expand contractions to test ("it is").
