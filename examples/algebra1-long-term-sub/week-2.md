### Opening (say this)

"Graphing works, but it's slow and answers like (2.5, 1.3) are hard to read. Substitution gives exact answers. If one equation tells us what y equals, we can REPLACE y in the other equation with that expression. Then we have one equation with one variable, which we already know how to solve."

### Do Now

1. Solve by graphing:  y = x + 1  and  y = −x + 3.
2. Write 3x + y = 2 in slope-intercept form. Then name the slope and y-intercept.
3. Is (3, 1) a solution of the system  x + y = 4  and  2x − y = 5?

### Mini-lesson: worked examples (script)

**Worked example 1:** Solve by substitution:  y = 2x + 3  and  3x + y = 8.

Write each step on the board and say what you're doing and why. Ask the question in italics before writing the step and give 3-5 seconds of think time.

1. The first equation already says y = 2x + 3.
2. Substitute (2x + 3) for y in the second equation, keeping the parentheses: 3x + (2x + 3) = 8
3. Distribute: 3x + 2x + 3 = 8
4. Combine like terms: 5x + 3 = 8
5. Subtract 3 from both sides: 5x = 5
6. Divide both sides by 5: x = 1
7. Substitute x = 1 into y = 2x + 3: y = 2(1) + 3 = 5
8. Solution (1, 5). Check in the second equation: 3(1) + (5) = 8 ✓

Say: *"So the answer is (1, 5). How do we know it's right?"* (We checked it in the original equations.)

**Worked example 2:** Solve by substitution:  y = −x + 2  and  2x − 3y = 14.

Write each step on the board and say what you're doing and why. Ask the question in italics before writing the step and give 3-5 seconds of think time.

1. The first equation already says y = −x + 2.
2. Substitute (−x + 2) for y in the second equation, keeping the parentheses: 2x − 3(−x + 2) = 14
3. Distribute: 2x + 3x − 6 = 14
4. Combine like terms: 5x − 6 = 14
5. Add 6 to both sides: 5x = 20
6. Divide both sides by 5: x = 4
7. Substitute x = 4 into y = −x + 2: y = −(4) + 2 = −2
8. Solution (4, −2). Check in the second equation: 2(4) − 3(−2) = 14 ✓

Say: *"So the answer is (4, −2). How do we know it's right?"* (We checked it in the original equations.)

**Find the mistake** (project or write this; ask students to find and explain the error with a partner for 2 minutes):

> Solve: y = −x + 4 and 2x − y = 5.  
> 2x − x + 4 = 5  
> x + 4 = 5  
> x = 1  

*What's wrong:* No parentheses, so the minus sign was only applied to the first term. 2x − (−x + 4) = 5 means 2x + x − 4 = 5.  
*Correct:* 3x − 4 = 5, x = 3, y = 1. Solution (3, 1).

### Watch for (common mistakes and what to say)

- **Forgetting parentheses when substituting.** Say: "Write the parentheses first, then put the expression inside."
- **Stopping after finding x.** Say: "A solution is an ordered pair. Go back and find y."
- **Substituting back into the same equation you used.** Say: "Use the 'y =' equation to find y; check in the other."

**Do Now 1.** Solve by substitution:  y = x + 2  and  x + y = 6.  
**Answer: (2, 4)**

**Quiz 1.** Solve by substitution:  y = 3x − 1  and  2x + y = 7.  
**Answer: (8/5, 19/5)**

**Quiz 2.** Solve by substitution:  x + 3y = 11  and  4x − y = 5.  
**Answer: (2, 3)**

**Quiz 4.** Solve by substitution:  y = 2x + 4  and  6x − 3y = 3.  
**Answer: no solution**
   - The first equation already says y = 2x + 4.
   - Substitute (2x + 4) for y in the second equation, keeping the parentheses: 6x − 3(2x + 4) = 3
   - Distribute: 6x − 6x − 12 = 3
   - Combine like terms: the x terms cancel, leaving −12 = 3
   - −12 = 3 is never true, so there is no solution (the lines are parallel).

**Quiz 5.** Solve by substitution:  y = x + 3  and  2x − 2y = −6.  
**Answer: infinitely many solutions**
   - The first equation already says y = x + 3.
   - Substitute (x + 3) for y in the second equation, keeping the parentheses: 2x − 2(x + 3) = −6
   - Distribute: 2x − 2x − 6 = −6
   - Combine like terms: the x terms cancel, leaving −6 = −6
   - −6 = −6 is always true, so every point on the line works: infinitely many solutions (the equations are the same line).
