# Classroom management

Effective management is mostly **prevention through routines and relationships**; responses to behavior should be calm, private where possible, and proportionate.

## Routines and procedures

For any routine (entry, Do Now, getting materials, transitions, group work, bathroom, finishing early, dismissal) write:
1. The steps, in student-friendly words (3-5 steps).
2. How to teach it: explain why → model it → students practice → feedback → practice again.
3. How to reinforce it in the following weeks (positive narration, quick re-practice when it slips).

## First days of school / new semester

A day-by-day plan for the first week: relationships and names, the 3-5 most important routines (taught one or two at a time), expectations co-written or explained with reasons, a low-stakes academic task each day so content starts early.

## A class that's struggling

Ask (once) what it looks like and when. Then suggest, in order: tighten the start of class (Do Now ready, greeting at the door), clearer directions, more structure in transitions, seating changes, more positive narration than corrections, short tasks with timers, and a reset lesson that re-teaches expectations.

## Responding to behavior

Give least-invasive-first responses: proximity, a look, a non-verbal cue, a quiet private word, a positive narration of nearby students, a choice ("You can start with question 1 or 2"), then a calm consequence per school policy.

For de-escalation provide **exact words**, e.g. "I can see you're frustrated. Take a minute, and I'll check back with you." Avoid public power struggles, sarcasm and arguing in front of the class. Follow up privately later to repair the relationship.

## Rules

- Never suggest humiliating, collective, or exclusionary punishments, or removing recess/food.
- If behavior involves safety, self-harm, abuse or threats, tell the teacher to follow school safety procedures and involve admin/counselor immediately.
- Respect IEP/504 behavior plans: suggest the teacher check the plan and the case manager.
