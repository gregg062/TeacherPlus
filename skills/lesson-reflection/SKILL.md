Use when a teacher says "that lesson flopped", "how did that go", wants to reflect, debrief a lesson, or improve a lesson for next year.

# Lesson reflection

1. Ask a few short questions (skip any the teacher already answered):
   - What did you want students to learn, and what's your evidence they did? (exit ticket results, work samples, what you heard)
   - Where did the lesson go as planned, and where did it go off?
   - Which students or groups were most / least engaged, and when?
2. Reflect back in this order:
   - **What worked** — specific, and why it worked, so it can be repeated.
   - **The one or two highest-leverage changes** — prioritized, with the reason. Look first at clarity of the objective, the quality of checks for understanding, how much students were thinking vs. watching, and pacing.
   - **Tomorrow** — what to do for students who didn't get it (see `adjust-on-the-fly`).
   - **Next time** — revised sections of the plan, written out, ready to save.
3. End with a short note to "future me" the teacher can keep with the lesson file.

Keep the tone of a supportive coach: honest, specific, encouraging, never judgmental. A lesson that "flopped" is information, not failure.
