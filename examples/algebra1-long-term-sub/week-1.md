### Mini-lesson: worked examples (script)

**Worked example 1:** Write 3x + y = 7 in slope-intercept form. Then name the slope and y-intercept.

Write each step on the board and say what you're doing and why. Ask the question in italics before writing the step and give 3-5 seconds of think time.

1. Start: 3x + y = 7
2. Subtract 3x from both sides: y = −3x + 7
3. Slope −3, y-intercept 7.

Say: *"So the answer is y = −3x + 7; slope −3, y-intercept 7. How do we know it's right?"* (We checked it in the original equations.)

**Worked example 2:** Write 4x + 2y = 10 in slope-intercept form. Then name the slope and y-intercept.

Write each step on the board and say what you're doing and why. Ask the question in italics before writing the step and give 3-5 seconds of think time.

1. Start: 4x + 2y = 10
2. Subtract 4x from both sides: 2y = −4x + 10
3. Divide every term on both sides by 2: y = −2x + 5
4. Slope −2, y-intercept 5.

Say: *"So the answer is y = −2x + 5; slope −2, y-intercept 5. How do we know it's right?"* (We checked it in the original equations.)

**Find the mistake** (project or write this; ask students to find and explain the error with a partner for 2 minutes):

> Write 6x + 3y = 12 in slope-intercept form.  
> 3y = 6x + 12  
> y = 2x + 4  

*What's wrong:* The student added 6x to the right side instead of subtracting it. To remove +6x from the left we subtract 6x from BOTH sides.  
*Correct:* 3y = −6x + 12, so y = −2x + 4.

### Watch for (common mistakes and what to say)

- **Slope and intercept swapped (graphing y = 3x + 2 starting at 3).** Say: "Which number is alone, without an x? That's where we start on the y-axis."
- **Dividing only the first term by the coefficient of y (y = −2x + 10).** Say: "Divide EVERY term on both sides, not just the first one."

### Opening (say this)

"A system is two equations about the same two unknowns. A solution is an ordered pair (x, y) that makes BOTH equations true at the same time. On a graph, that's where the two lines cross."

### Do Now

1. Graph y = x − 2. List three points on the line.
2. Write 3x + y = 4 in slope-intercept form. Then name the slope and y-intercept.
3. Identify the slope and y-intercept of y = −2x + 7.

### Mini-lesson: worked examples (script)

**Worked example 1:** Is (2, 3) a solution of the system  x + y = 5  and  2x − y = 1?

Write each step on the board and say what you're doing and why. Ask the question in italics before writing the step and give 3-5 seconds of think time.

1. Equation 1: x + y = 5. Substitute: (2) + (3) = 5. Equals 5 ✓
2. Equation 2: 2x − y = 1. Substitute: 2(2) − (3) = 1. Equals 1 ✓
3. It works in both equations, so it is a solution.

Say: *"So the answer is Yes. How do we know it's right?"* (We checked it in the original equations.)

**Worked example 2:** Is (1, 3) a solution of the system  y = 2x + 1  and  x + y = 6?

Write each step on the board and say what you're doing and why. Ask the question in italics before writing the step and give 3-5 seconds of think time.

1. Equation 1: y = 2x + 1. Substitute: 3 = 2(1) + 1 = 3. True ✓
2. Equation 2: x + y = 6. Substitute: (1) + (3) = 4. Not 6 ✗
3. It must work in BOTH equations to be a solution.

Say: *"So the answer is No. How do we know it's right?"* (We checked it in the original equations.)

**Find the mistake** (project or write this; ask students to find and explain the error with a partner for 2 minutes):

> Is (4, 1) a solution of x + y = 5 and x − y = 2?  
> 4 + 1 = 5 ✓  
> So (4, 1) is a solution.  

*What's wrong:* The student only checked the first equation. 4 − 1 = 3, not 2, so it fails the second equation.  
*Correct:* No, (4, 1) is not a solution; it must work in both equations.

### Watch for (common mistakes and what to say)

- **Checking only one equation.** Say: "A solution has to make BOTH equations true. Check every equation."
- **Substituting x and y in the wrong order.** Say: "Ordered pair means (x, y): x first, always."

### Opening (say this)

### Mini-lesson: worked examples (script)

**Worked example 1:** Solve by graphing:  y = x + 1  and  y = −x + 5.

Write each step on the board and say what you're doing and why. Ask the question in italics before writing the step and give 3-5 seconds of think time.

1. Graph y = x + 1: start at (0, 1), slope 1.
2. Graph y = −x + 5: start at (0, 5), slope −1.
3. The lines cross at (2, 3).
4. Check in both: 3 = (2) + 1 ✓ and 3 = −(2) + 5 ✓

Say: *"So the answer is (2, 3). How do we know it's right?"* (We checked it in the original equations.)

**Worked example 2:** Solve by graphing:  y = 2x − 3  and  y = −(1/2)x + 2.

Write each step on the board and say what you're doing and why. Ask the question in italics before writing the step and give 3-5 seconds of think time.

1. Graph y = 2x − 3: start at (0, −3), slope 2.
2. Graph y = −(1/2)x + 2: start at (0, 2), slope −1/2.
3. The lines cross at (2, 1).
4. Check in both: 1 = 2(2) − 3 ✓ and 1 = −1/2(2) + 2 ✓

Say: *"So the answer is (2, 1). How do we know it's right?"* (We checked it in the original equations.)

**Find the mistake** (project or write this; ask students to find and explain the error with a partner for 2 minutes):

> Solve by graphing: y = x + 1 and y = −x + 5.  
> The lines cross at (3, 2).  

*What's wrong:* The student read the point backwards. The lines cross where x = 2 and y = 3. Check: 3 = 2 + 1 ✓ and 3 = −2 + 5 ✓.  
*Correct:* (2, 3)

### Watch for (common mistakes and what to say)

- **Reading the intersection as (y, x).** Say: "Go across first for x, then up or down for y."
- **Sloppy graphs giving the wrong point.** Say: "Use a ruler, and ALWAYS check the point in both equations."
- **Thinking parallel lines have a solution.** Say: "If the lines never cross, there's no point that works in both: no solution."
