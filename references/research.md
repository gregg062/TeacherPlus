# TeacherPlus evidence base

The methods TeacherPlus skills use, the research behind them, and where each is applied. This file is for reviewers and for teachers who ask "why does it do it this way?".

> **Review status: DRAFT.** Compiled by the developer and Claude, awaiting review by the educator reviewer. Each summary is a short, plain-language reading of the source; please check them against the originals and correct or remove anything that overstates the evidence. Strength ratings: **Strong** = consistent findings across many studies/meta-analyses; **Moderate** = good studies but narrower or mixed; **Practice-based** = widely used expert practice with limited direct experimental evidence.

## Instruction

**Explicit instruction and Rosenshine's principles** — Strong
Rosenshine, B. (2012). Principles of instruction: Research-based strategies that all teachers should know. *American Educator, 36*(1), 12–19, 39.
Ten principles drawn from cognitive science, studies of master teachers and of cognitive supports: begin with a short review; present new material in small steps with practice after each; ask many questions and check the responses of all students; provide models (worked examples); guide student practice; check for understanding; obtain a high success rate; provide scaffolds for difficult tasks; require independent practice; engage in weekly and monthly review.
*Used in:* lesson-delivery, lesson-planning, lesson-review, long-term-sub-plans.

**Gradual release of responsibility ("I do, we do, you do")** — Moderate
Pearson, P. D., & Gallagher, M. C. (1983). The instruction of reading comprehension. *Contemporary Educational Psychology, 8*(3), 317–344.
Fisher, D., & Frey, N. (2013). *Better learning through structured teaching: A framework for the gradual release of responsibility* (2nd ed.). ASCD.
Responsibility moves from teacher modeling to guided practice to collaborative and independent work.
*Used in:* lesson-delivery run of show, long-term-sub-plans daily routine.

**Worked examples, cognitive load and fading** — Strong
Sweller, J., & Cooper, G. A. (1985). The use of worked examples as a substitute for problem solving in learning algebra. *Cognition and Instruction, 2*(1), 59–89.
Sweller, J. (1988). Cognitive load during problem solving: Effects on learning. *Cognitive Science, 12*(2), 257–285.
Renkl, A., & Atkinson, R. K. (2003). Structuring the transition from example study to problem solving in cognitive skill acquisition. *Educational Psychologist, 38*(1), 15–22.
Kalyuga, S., Ayres, P., Chandler, P., & Sweller, J. (2003). The expertise reversal effect. *Educational Psychologist, 38*(1), 23–31.
Novices learn more from studying worked examples than from solving equivalent problems unaided; support should be faded as expertise grows (and heavy guidance can hinder students who are already proficient).
*Used in:* lesson-delivery (think-alouds), differentiation (worked → faded examples), adjust-on-the-fly.

**Algebra instruction (IES practice guide)** — Moderate to Strong (per recommendation)
Star, J. R., Caronongan, P., Foegen, A., Furgeson, J., Keating, B., Larson, M. R., Lyskawa, J., McCallum, W. G., Porath, J., & Zbiek, R. M. (2015). *Teaching strategies for improving algebra knowledge in middle and high school students* (NCEE 2014-4333). U.S. Department of Education, Institute of Education Sciences.
Recommendations: use solved problems to engage students in analyzing algebraic reasoning and strategies; teach students to use the structure of algebraic representations; teach students to intentionally choose from alternative strategies when solving problems.
*Used in:* long-term-sub-plans (Algebra example), checks-for-understanding (error analysis), references/misconceptions/algebra-1.md.

**Learning from incorrect examples (error analysis)** — Moderate
Booth, J. L., Lange, K. E., Koedinger, K. R., & Newton, K. J. (2013). Using example problems to improve student learning in algebra: Differentiating between correct and incorrect examples. *Learning and Instruction, 25*, 24–34.
Having students explain why an incorrect worked example is wrong improved conceptual understanding of algebra.
*Used in:* "find the mistake" tasks in lesson-delivery, checks-for-understanding and the Algebra example.

## Memory and practice

**Retrieval practice** — Strong
Roediger, H. L., & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). Test-enhanced learning: Taking memory tests improves long-term retention. *Psychological Science, 17*(3), 249–255.
Dunlosky, J., Rawson, K. A., Marsh, E. J., Nathan, M. J., & Willingham, D. T. (2013). Improving students' learning with effective learning techniques. *Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14*(1), 4–58.
Practice testing (low-stakes recall) produces better long-term retention than re-studying; Dunlosky et al. rate practice testing and distributed practice as the two highest-utility techniques.
*Used in:* Do Now design, checks-for-understanding, unit-planner spiral review.

**Spaced (distributed) practice and interleaving** — Strong (spacing); Moderate (interleaving)
Cepeda, N. J., Pashler, H., Vul, E., Wixted, J. T., & Rohrer, D. (2006). Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis. *Psychological Bulletin, 132*(3), 354–380.
Pashler, H., Bain, P., Bottge, B., Graesser, A., Koedinger, K., McDaniel, M., & Metcalfe, J. (2007). *Organizing instruction and study to improve student learning* (NCER 2007-2004). Institute of Education Sciences.
Spreading practice over time beats massing it; mixing problem types helps students learn to choose strategies.
*Used in:* Do Now (yesterday + 2–4 weeks ago), curriculum-map spiral, mixed-practice days in the Algebra example.

## Assessment and feedback

**Formative assessment** — Strong
Black, P., & Wiliam, D. (1998). Assessment and classroom learning. *Assessment in Education: Principles, Policy & Practice, 5*(1), 7–74.
Black, P., & Wiliam, D. (1998). Inside the black box: Raising standards through classroom assessment. *Phi Delta Kappan, 80*(2), 139–148.
Wiliam, D. (2011). *Embedded formative assessment*. Solution Tree.
Using evidence of student learning during instruction to adjust teaching improves achievement. Wiliam describes hinge questions (quick diagnostic questions whose wrong answers reveal specific misconceptions) and all-student response systems.
*Used in:* checks-for-understanding, adjust-on-the-fly, item-analysis, lesson-delivery.

**Feedback** — Strong
Hattie, J., & Timperley, H. (2007). The power of feedback. *Review of Educational Research, 77*(1), 81–112.
Effective feedback answers: Where am I going? How am I going? Where to next? Feedback about the task and process is more useful than praise about the person.
*Used in:* rubric-builder, observation-feedback, report-comments.

**Rubrics** — Practice-based / Moderate
Brookhart, S. M. (2013). *How to create and use rubrics for formative assessment and grading*. ASCD.
Good rubrics describe observable qualities of the work at each level, tied to learning goals, rather than counting features or judging effort.
*Used in:* rubric-builder.

**Multiple-choice item writing** — Moderate (consensus guidelines)
Haladyna, T. M., Downing, S. M., & Rodriguez, M. C. (2002). A review of multiple-choice item-writing guidelines for classroom assessment. *Applied Measurement in Education, 15*(3), 309–333.
Guidelines include: plausible distractors based on common errors, avoid "all/none of the above", keep options similar in length, avoid negatives and clues.
*Used in:* assessment-builder, assessment-review, checks-for-understanding.

**Depth of Knowledge / cognitive rigor** — Practice-based (alignment framework)
Webb, N. L. (1997). *Criteria for alignment of expectations and assessments in mathematics and science education* (Research Monograph No. 6). National Institute for Science Education.
Hess, K. K., Jones, B. S., Carlock, D., & Walkup, J. R. (2009). Cognitive rigor: Blending the strengths of Bloom's taxonomy and Webb's depth of knowledge to enhance classroom-level processes. ERIC ED517804.
Anderson, L. W., & Krathwohl, D. R. (Eds.). (2001). *A taxonomy for learning, teaching, and assessing*. Longman.
A way to judge the complexity of thinking a standard or task demands (recall → skill/concept → strategic thinking → extended thinking).
*Used in:* standards-alignment, assessment-builder blueprints, assessment-review.

## Curriculum design

**Backward design (Understanding by Design)** — Practice-based (widely adopted framework)
Wiggins, G., & McTighe, J. (2005). *Understanding by design* (Expanded 2nd ed.). ASCD.
Plan from desired results → acceptable evidence → learning experiences.
*Used in:* unit-planner, curriculum-map, long-term-sub-plans.

## Differentiation and access

**Universal Design for Learning** — Practice-based framework
CAST. (2018). *Universal Design for Learning Guidelines version 2.2*. http://udlguidelines.cast.org
Provide multiple means of engagement, representation, and action and expression.
*Used in:* differentiation, leveled-texts.

**Differentiated instruction** — Practice-based
Tomlinson, C. A. (2014). *The differentiated classroom: Responding to the needs of all learners* (2nd ed.). ASCD.
Adjust content, process and product to readiness, interest and learning profile while keeping the same essential learning goals.
*Used in:* differentiation, leveled-texts.

**Text complexity** — Practice-based (standards guidance)
Common Core State Standards for English Language Arts, Appendix A: Research supporting key elements of the standards (2010).
Students need regular, supported access to grade-level complex text, not only text at their independent level. TeacherPlus therefore treats leveled texts as a bridge to grade-level text, not a replacement.
*Used in:* leveled-texts.

## Classroom talk and engagement

**Wait time** — Moderate
Rowe, M. B. (1986). Wait time: Slowing down may be a way of speeding up! *Journal of Teacher Education, 37*(1), 43–50.
Pausing 3+ seconds after a question increases the length and quality of student responses and the number of students responding.
*Used in:* lesson-delivery questioning, discussion-and-engagement.

**Accountable talk** — Moderate
Michaels, S., O'Connor, C., & Resnick, L. B. (2008). Deliberative discourse idealized and realized: Accountable talk in the classroom and in civic life. *Studies in Philosophy and Education, 27*(4), 283–297.
Productive discussion is accountable to the learning community, to accurate knowledge, and to rigorous thinking; teacher talk moves ("Say more", "Who can add on?") support it.
*Used in:* discussion-and-engagement.

**Cooperative learning** — Strong
Johnson, D. W., & Johnson, R. T. (2009). An educational psychology success story: Social interdependence theory and cooperative learning. *Educational Researcher, 38*(5), 365–379.
Group work is effective when it has positive interdependence and individual accountability.
*Used in:* discussion-and-engagement (roles, individual products).

**Teaching techniques (cold call, everybody writes, etc.)** — Practice-based
Lemov, D. (2021). *Teach like a champion 3.0*. Jossey-Bass.
Techniques drawn from observing high-performing teachers.
*Used in:* lesson-delivery, discussion-and-engagement, classroom-management.

## Classroom management

**Evidence-based classroom management** — Strong (reviews)
Simonsen, B., Fairbanks, S., Briesch, A., Myers, D., & Sugai, G. (2008). Evidence-based practices in classroom management: Considerations for research to practice. *Education and Treatment of Children, 31*(3), 351–380.
Supported practices: maximize structure; post, teach and review expectations; actively engage students; acknowledge appropriate behavior; respond to inappropriate behavior with a continuum of strategies.
*Used in:* classroom-management.

## Early warning indicators

**Attendance, behavior and course performance ("ABC") indicators** — Strong (predictive research)
Balfanz, R., Herzog, L., & Mac Iver, D. J. (2007). Preventing student disengagement and keeping students on the graduation path in urban middle-grades schools: Early identification and effective interventions. *Educational Psychologist, 42*(4), 223–235.
Allensworth, E. M., & Easton, J. Q. (2005). *The on-track indicator as a predictor of high school graduation*. Consortium on Chicago School Research.
Low attendance, failing core courses and behavior problems in the middle grades and 9th grade strongly predict later dropping out.
*Used in:* at-risk screening rules and the at-risk-review skill.

**Chronic absence = missing 10% or more of school days** — Standard definition
Attendance Works (attendanceworks.org); also used by the U.S. Department of Education.
*Used in:* the 90% attendance threshold.

## Coaching, feedback to teachers and collaboration

**Instructional coaching** — Strong (meta-analysis)
Kraft, M. A., Blazar, D., & Hogan, D. (2018). The effect of teacher coaching on instruction and achievement: A meta-analysis of the causal evidence. *Review of Educational Research, 88*(4), 547–588.
Coaching has positive effects on teachers' instructional practice and smaller positive effects on student achievement.
Knight, J. (2018). *The impact cycle*. Corwin. (Identify → learn → improve cycle; practice-based.)
*Used in:* coaching-cycle, observation-feedback.

**Bite-sized action steps and practice** — Practice-based
Bambrick-Santoyo, P. (2016). *Get better faster: A 90-day plan for coaching new teachers*. Jossey-Bass.
Bambrick-Santoyo, P. (2018). *Leverage leadership 2.0*. Jossey-Bass.
One high-leverage, observable action step at a time, rehearsed before it's used with students.
*Used in:* observation-feedback, new-teacher-mentoring, lesson-review ("one or two changes").

**Professional learning communities and protocols** — Practice-based
DuFour, R., DuFour, R., Eaker, R., Many, T. W., & Mattos, M. (2016). *Learning by doing: A handbook for professional learning communities at work* (3rd ed.). Solution Tree.
School Reform Initiative. Protocols (tuning protocol, looking at student work). schoolreforminitiative.org
*Used in:* department-leadership.

**Induction and mentoring for new teachers** — Moderate
Ingersoll, R. M., & Strong, M. (2011). The impact of induction and mentoring programs for beginning teachers: A critical review of the research. *Review of Educational Research, 81*(2), 201–233.
*Used in:* new-teacher-mentoring.

**Reflective practice** — Practice-based
Schön, D. A. (1983). *The reflective practitioner*. Basic Books.
*Used in:* lesson-reflection.

## Where evidence is thin

- **Long-term substitute planning** has little direct research. The long-term-sub-plans skill applies the instructional research above (consistent routines, explicit instruction, worked examples, retrieval, formative checks) plus common district practice.
- **Learning styles** (matching instruction to "visual/auditory/kinesthetic" learners) is **not** supported by research and TeacherPlus does not use it (Pashler, H., McDaniel, M., Rohrer, D., & Bjork, R. (2008). Learning styles: Concepts and evidence. *Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 9*(3), 105–119).
