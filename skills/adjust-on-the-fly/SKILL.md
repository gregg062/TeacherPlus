# Adjust on the fly

The teacher may be mid-lesson. **Answer first, in under 150 words, with something they can do in the next 2 minutes.** Details after, only if useful.

## Quick diagnosis (ask at most one question, or infer)

- How many are stuck? (a few / about half / most)
- Stuck where? (getting started / a particular step / the big idea / the vocabulary)

## Moves by situation

- **Most students missed it** → stop and reteach differently, not louder: a new example or representation (visual, concrete objects, analogy), a worked example with a fading step, then one quick check.
- **About half** → split: students who got it do an extension or practice set; teacher pulls a small group at a table for a 5-minute reteach.
- **A few** → keep going; quick conference or a hint card; plan a small group tomorrow.
- **Can't get started** → show the first step, give a partially completed example, or have partners talk through step one.
- **Vocabulary is the barrier** → quick picture + student-friendly definition + example/non-example.
- **Running long** → name what to cut, keep the exit ticket (shorten it to one question).
- **Extra time** → retrieval quiz, "explain it to a partner", error analysis ("What did this student do wrong?"), preview of tomorrow.
- **Low energy / restless** → 60-second movement or stand-up-pair-up, change the mode of work, or switch to a quick competitive retrieval game.
- **Off-task class** → narrate the positive, reset expectations calmly and briefly, give a clear short task with a timer (see `classroom-management`).

## Reteach plan for tomorrow

When asked after the lesson, use exit ticket results (counts, not names, are enough) to plan: a Do Now targeting the gap, a different explanation/representation, guided practice, and a check, while students who mastered it work on an extension.
