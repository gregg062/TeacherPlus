# Fractions (grades 3–6) — common misconceptions

Status: DRAFT, awaiting educator review.

### Bigger denominator means bigger fraction
- **Looks like:** 1/8 > 1/4.
- **Why:** whole-number thinking (8 > 4).
- **Response:** fair-share story (one pizza shared by 8 vs. 4 people); fraction strips.
- **Hinge distractor:** 1/8 is greater.

### Adding across numerators and denominators
- **Looks like:** 1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5.
- **Why:** applies the multiplication pattern to addition; treats fractions as two separate whole numbers.
- **Response:** estimation: 1/2 + 1/3 must be more than 1/2, and 2/5 is less than 1/2. Use a number line or area model.

### Fractions are not numbers / not on a number line
- **Looks like:** can't place 3/4 between 0 and 1; thinks 5/4 can't exist.
- **Response:** number line work early and often; improper fractions as positions past 1.

### Equal parts not required
- **Looks like:** shading 1 of 3 unequal parts as 1/3.
- **Response:** non-examples; "Would you be happy with that piece?"

### Multiplying always makes bigger, dividing always smaller
- **Looks like:** 6 × 1/2 = 12; surprised that 3 ÷ 1/2 = 6.
- **Response:** "How many halves fit in 3?" with drawings; 6 groups of one half.

### Comparing by the gap between numerator and denominator
- **Looks like:** 3/4 = 5/6 "because both are one piece away from a whole".
- **Response:** the pieces are different sizes; benchmark fractions and common denominators.

### Decimal length
- **Looks like:** 0.25 > 0.5 because 25 > 5; 0.100 > 0.2.
- **Response:** place value chart; money (25¢ vs 50¢); grid models.
