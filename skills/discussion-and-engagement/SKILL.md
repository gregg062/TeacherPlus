# Discussion & engagement

## 1. Pick the right structure

Match structure to purpose and explain the choice in one line:

| Purpose | Good structures |
|---|---|
| Quick processing during instruction | Turn-and-talk, think-pair-share, stop-and-jot |
| Everyone accountable for an answer | All-write then cold call, mini-whiteboards, numbered heads |
| Deep text/idea discussion | Socratic seminar, fishbowl, Save the Last Word |
| Taking and defending positions | Four corners, philosophical chairs, structured academic controversy |
| Learning from each other | Jigsaw, gallery walk, carousel brainstorm |
| Practice and feedback | Pair problem-solving with roles (driver/navigator), quiz-quiz-trade |

## 2. Plan it fully

- **The question(s)**: open, worth arguing about, answerable from evidence the students have. Give a question sequence: entry question everyone can answer → core question → stretch question.
- **Setup**: grouping (size and how chosen), roles with one-line job descriptions (facilitator, evidence-finder, summarizer, timekeeper), seating/movement, timings.
- **Directions** to say/post: what, how long, what done looks like.
- **Accountability**: what each student writes or produces, and how the teacher hears from every group.
- **Sentence stems** at two levels (e.g. "I agree with ___ because..." / "Building on ___'s point, the evidence suggests..."), plus stems for disagreeing respectfully.
- **Teacher moves while it runs**: what to listen for, prompts to push thinking ("Say more", "What's your evidence?", "Who sees it differently?"), and how to bring it back to whole class.
- **Debrief**: 2-3 questions that connect the discussion to the learning target.

## Participation rules of thumb

- Think/write time before talk, so quieter students and multilingual learners have something to say.
- Use structures, not volunteers: cold call warmly, after think time, and make "I don't know yet" safe ("Who can help? ... Now you try").
- Norms for respectful talk taught and posted; groups of 2-4.
- For multilingual learners: allow home-language discussion first, provide visuals and stems. For students with anxiety or IEP-related needs: offer rehearsal (tell them the question first) or written participation.
